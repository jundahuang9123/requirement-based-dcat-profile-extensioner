\section{Discussion and Threats to Validity}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{The reference delta as one modeling, partly outside the atoms.}
A published profile records one team's modeling at one time against one base release, and several modelings can
meet an atom, so relaxed matching and the adjudication of unmatched changes carry weight; Strict and Recovery
bracket this judgment.
Reference elements that no atom asks for lie beyond the reach of an atom-driven step and are not counted as missed
(Sect.~\ref{sec:problem}); elements that loosen the base are kept apart, and references extracted by hand add the
extractor's judgment.
Where a published profile mints nothing, every mint departs from the reference, although it may model its atom
validly.

\paragraph{From extracted needs to a profile.}
The reference atoms isolate the modeling step: the companion study's curator reviewed each, and no error of its
workflow reaches them \cite{anon2027rq1}.
The extracted atoms come from needs that workflow retained and no person accepted, far more than the reference has
atoms, so running the pipeline on them shows how extraction errors reach the profile.

\paragraph{Minting and review cost.}
The extension rate shows how often the pipeline mints.
A term outside the index fails V1, so where the vocabularies a domain reuses are missing, the model must mint or
choose another term.
The rules baseline mints at a confidence of 0.45, below the threshold of 0.5, so each of its mints waits for a % src: @5ffd56e B/llm/mock.py:199; B/contracts.py:599
person; a model's mint that passes every check is auto-accepted on its self-reported confidence.
FP's extension rate on the test cases is \res{test}{FP}{extrate}, that of the reference deltas
\res{test}{all}{extrateRef}.

Each mint costs a reviewer decision, queued or not, because only the team can commit its namespace to a new term:
the extension rate is a cost in use, not only a measure of reuse.

\paragraph{Retrieval and the vocabularies within reach.}
Retrieval is lexical: an atom in plain words can miss a term documented in other words, and the closed candidate list
then pushes the model toward a mint; class retrieval cuts its raw hits before it boosts base classes, which dropped a
base class in a probe on a synthetic atom.
The index, the base's terms, all \ac{DCAT} terms and 12 other vocabularies, bounds what can be reused, as the corpus bounds what % src: paper/inputs/tool-facts-main-5ffd56e.txt:2-3; digests/dcat-state.md:122 (all 60 DCAT terms); main-wt README.md:122-126
the companion workflow finds \cite{anon2027rq1}, and extending it changes the outcome.
It holds neither the RDF Data Cube vocabulary \cite{w3cdatacube} nor \ac{DQV} \cite{w3cdqv}, whereas StatDCAT-AP mints
four properties, two of them with Data Cube classes as ranges, and reuses \ac{DQV} for quality annotations % src: statdcatap PDF (references.bib:55), Annex II, p. 52 (stat:attribute, stat:dimension, stat:numSeries, stat:statUnitMeasure; dqv:hasQualityAnnotation); digests/pilot.md:369-386
\cite{statdcatap}: the pipeline can mint those two only with other ranges, and must mint where the publication
reuses.

\paragraph{Machine judgments and the final say.}
Dispositions, attribution verdicts and confidence are model judgments: none has been verified, and none is a
person's acceptance.
The judge is the proposer's model, so it is not independent of the proposer, and \ac{LLM} judges are biased and
inconsistent \cite{zheng2023judge,wang2024fair,stureborg2024inconsistent,liu2023geval}.
It judges only changes without a symbolic error, its verdict blocks only when it finds the change unsupported or
names an unsupported facet, and it is not asked again after consolidation, yet it is the only check that reads facets
other than the obligation against the atoms.
B1 checks that the named base elements exist and are strong enough, not that they mean what the atom asks; nothing
checks out-of-scope decisions; and the confidence threshold is our setting.

\paragraph{The contribution of verification and repair.}
The ablations remove retrieval, repair and the judge in turn, but not verification, whose switch is defined and
never read, so what the symbolic checks contribute as a whole is not measured.

for, each get a duplicate error and go to repair, where the repairer may rewrite or withdraw them, before
consolidation would have merged them.

\paragraph{What conformance shows.}
Validated together with the official shapes, the delta cannot relax them for any instance (Sect.~\ref{sec:problem});
the rules test what this leaves open, within limits.
C7 is a satisfiability witness, not a proof of conformance: it builds one positive instance per class, without
inference, its branch for data only the base rejects cannot fire, and it warns on legal refinements (a range narrowed
to a known subclass, a minimum of two on a literal, a datatype outside its samples) and at its depth limit, so a C7 % src: digests/issues-at-main.md:49 (K1, probe P11); digests/knowledge-compile-ui.md:466-490; @5ffd56e B/conformance/check.py:195, 218-225
warning is a lead, not a defect.
No negative witnesses are built, and controlled-vocabulary usage levels are not modeled.
A range counts as narrowed only through subclass axioms the index knows, and an incomparable range counts as
tightened without an item in the report; C1 compares obligations only where the base has a shape, while V3 also
compares those the specification lists without one, and where the release data of the 1.2.1 to 2.1.1 bases give no % src: paper/inputs/tool-facts-main-5ffd56e.txt:17-21 (unknown obligations); digests/issues-at-main.md:116-121 (NEW-4)
obligation, a loosening goes unseen.

\paragraph{Contamination.}
The model may have seen the published profiles, their shapes and their documents in training
\cite{sainz2023contamination}.
The closed book shows what it produces without atoms, which neither proves nor excludes that it saw a publication,
and counterfactual atoms test whether the output follows the atoms or the publication \cite{wu2024reciting}.
